# PortServDeliver

Data for the paper: 

Still Changing After All These Years”: A Dynamic Analysis of Portuguese Local Government Service Delivery Choices (1999-2025)

António Tavares * & Pedro Camões ±
* Research Center in Political Science (CICP), School of Economics, Management and Political Science, University of Minho, Portugal
± Research Unit on Governance, Competitiveness and Public Policies (GOVCOPP), University of Aveiro, Portugal

Abstract
This paper revisits Portuguese municipal corporatization as a dynamic delegation choice rather than a one-off shift from bureaucracy to corporation. Using a municipality-service-cohort panel for 278 mainland municipalities from 1999 to 2025, organized into nine three-year cohorts, it examines when local governments use municipally owned corporations, when they enter or exit corporatized provision, and how the 2012 legal-fiscal tightening reshaped those choices. The evidence shows that corporatization expanded through the late 2000s, narrowed after reform, and became increasingly concentrated in infrastructure and utility-like services. Fixed-effects status, entry, and exit models support a revised political transaction-cost account. Asset specificity is the most robust service predictor, while measurability operates less consistently than standard monitoring arguments imply. The strongest regularity is own-service persistence: once a service is corporatized, it tends to remain so, whereas cross-service spillovers are weak. Fiscal conditions matter more than partisan control, and exits are largely selective rather than random.
